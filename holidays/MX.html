Public Holidays in Mexico 2026

9 public and regional holidays observed across Mexico in 2026.

Mexico observes a compact list of official public holidays under its Federal Labour Law, several of which are deliberately moved to the nearest Monday to create long weekends known as "puentes." The calendar mixes patriotic dates — Independence Day, Revolution Day — with civic tributes to historical figures like Benito Juárez. Notably, widely celebrated cultural dates like Día de los Muertos and Cinco de Mayo are not official nationwide public holidays. Below is the full 2026 calendar.

What Each Holiday Means

Año Nuevo
The first day of the Gregorian calendar year, marked with fireworks, family gatherings, and a fresh start after the festive season.
February 2, 2026Monday
Día de la Constitución
Marks the anniversary of the 1917 Mexican Constitution, observed on the first Monday of February.
March 16, 2026Monday
Natalicio de Benito Juárez
Honours the birthday of Benito Juárez, one of Mexico's most celebrated former presidents.
April 2, 2026Thursday
Jueves Santo
Commemorates the Last Supper and marks the start of the Easter Triduum in the Christian calendar.
April 3, 2026Friday
Viernes Santo
A Christian observance marking the crucifixion of Jesus Christ, held on the Friday before Easter Sunday.
May 1, 2026Friday
Día del Trabajo
Honours workers and the labour movement, held nationwide on 1 May.
September 16, 2026Wednesday
Día de la Independencia
Commemorates the start of Mexico's War of Independence against Spain in 1810, celebrated with El Grito ceremonies.
November 16, 2026Monday
Día de la Revolución
Commemorates the start of the Mexican Revolution in 1910, observed on the third Monday of November.
December 25, 2026Friday
